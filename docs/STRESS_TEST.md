# Stress Test

This document identifies failure cases that reveal where the Access Orientation Protocol breaks or degrades. These are structural stress cases, not edge-case bugs.

## False self resonance

A system reports calm, clarity, or readiness while actually operating from distortion, fear, ideology, exhaustion, or dissociation.

### Failure effect
The entire loop inherits a false starting condition. Access points, assemblies, and movements may appear coherent while being anchored in misread internal state.

### Consequence
The protocol becomes internally elegant but externally unreliable.

## Skipped self resonance

A system begins directly from a problem statement, demand, or command without calibrating its actual internal state.

### Failure effect
The entry boundary is bypassed. The cycle may still produce output, but it does so without verified internal grounding.

### Consequence
Later stages become vulnerable to drift, reactivity, and false certainty.

## Assembly without transition

A system gathers relevant material and even achieves partial orientation but never crosses into a new operative state.

### Failure effect
The loop stalls in preparation. The system becomes informationally dense but behaviorally unchanged.

### Consequence
Assembly degrades into accumulation and orientation degrades into contemplation without movement.

## Abrupt transition

A system forces a state change too quickly, often under pressure to perform, decide, or act.

### Failure effect
Continuity breaks. The apparent transition hides unintegrated material that later reappears as instability, backlash, or collapse.

### Consequence
Movement may happen, but it is not supported by integrity.

## Movement without orientation

A system acts before restoring coordinates and direction.

### Failure effect
Movement becomes reactive activity rather than centered action.

### Consequence
The system may produce visible motion while increasing drift.

## Illusory trajectory

A system interprets a few actions or isolated outcomes as a stable pattern over time.

### Failure effect
Trajectory is confused with sequence. Pattern judgment is made without sufficient temporal depth.

### Consequence
The system overlearns from noise and may reinforce an unstable path.

## Closed loop without world resonance

A system declares completion because it feels internally resolved, because stakeholders agree, or because execution has finished.

### Failure effect
The completion boundary is faked. External reality has not yet confirmed, corrected, or contradicted the cycle.

### Consequence
The loop is closed symbolically but remains open in reality.

## Cross-cutting observation

Most failures in this protocol arise from one of three distortions:
- replacing calibration with assumption;
- replacing continuity with force;
- replacing reality feedback with internal closure.

These stress cases should be used as review prompts whenever the protocol appears smooth but results remain weak.

## Typical recovery moves

When one of these failures is detected, the preferred response is not acceleration but loop repair:
- reopen **Resonance (Self)** before advancing;
- reduce scope until a real **Access Point** becomes visible;
- refuse forced **Transition** when continuity is broken;
- suspend claims about **Trajectory** until enough time and evidence exist;
- seek **Resonance (World)** before declaring completion.
