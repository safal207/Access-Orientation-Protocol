# Architecture

This document describes the architecture of the Access Orientation Protocol stage by stage. The protocol is a loop. It begins with internal calibration and is only complete when the world answers back.

## 0. Resonance (Self)

### Purpose
Establish contact with the system's actual internal state before choosing an entry point or taking action.

### Inputs
- present internal state;
- available self-observation;
- signs of tension, drift, fragmentation, urgency, or clarity.

### Outputs
- a calibrated reading of current state;
- a clearer sense of limits, readiness, and distortion risks;
- permission or refusal to proceed into the cycle.

Because this is the entry boundary, distortions here propagate forward into every later stage of the cycle.

### Risks
- confusing desired state with actual state;
- mistaking performance, ideology, or momentum for resonance;
- entering the loop while fragmented or overloaded.

### Anti-patterns
- starting from urgency alone;
- performing self-description without calibration;
- treating self-resonance as optional.

## 1. Access Point

### Purpose
Identify the true entry or anchor point from which the cycle should proceed.

### Inputs
- calibrated internal state;
- active problem, question, tension, or opportunity;
- known constraints and boundary conditions.

### Outputs
- a chosen anchor point;
- a statement of what is actually being entered;
- reduced ambiguity about where to begin.

### Risks
- choosing a convenient rather than a true point of entry;
- anchoring on symptoms instead of structural causes;
- entering through inherited assumptions.

### Anti-patterns
- premature solutioning;
- beginning from the loudest signal rather than the most real one;
- using goals as substitutes for anchors.

## 2. Assembly

### Purpose
Collect the minimum set of relevant elements required for real orientation.

### Inputs
- access point;
- context fragments;
- observations, constraints, actors, resources, and signals.

### Outputs
- a coherent set of relevant elements;
- exclusions of non-resonant or distracting material;
- a workable frame for orientation.

### Risks
- accumulation without discrimination;
- confusing volume with relevance;
- building a collection that cannot support transition.

### Anti-patterns
- gathering everything because uncertainty feels unsafe;
- treating assembly as analysis completion;
- mistaking documentation density for readiness.

## 3. Orientation

### Purpose
Restore internal coordinates, relations, and direction so the system can understand where it is, what matters, and how parts relate.

### Inputs
- assembled relevant elements;
- current constraints and boundaries;
- remembered aims, roles, and dependencies.

### Outputs
- a coherent internal map;
- clarified directionality;
- recognition of tensions, tradeoffs, and viable next movement.

### Risks
- collapsing orientation into planning or task routing;
- imposing a map that ignores present conditions;
- mistaking certainty for alignment.

### Anti-patterns
- converting orientation into a checklist;
- replacing coordinates with slogans;
- optimizing tasks before restoring direction.

## 4. Transition

### Purpose
Move from one operative state to another without rupture, preserving continuity and integrity.

### Inputs
- coherent orientation;
- threshold conditions for change;
- state-dependent commitments, boundaries, and energy.

### Outputs
- a new operative state;
- continuity between previous and current conditions;
- readiness for movement.

### Risks
- forcing a state change before it is supported;
- abrupt rupture that creates hidden instability;
- pseudo-transition in which language changes but state does not.

### Anti-patterns
- jumping from orientation directly into output pressure;
- treating transition as a ceremonial label;
- confusing escalation with transformation.

## 5. Movement

### Purpose
Act from centered orientation rather than panic, inertia, imitation, or compulsion.

### Inputs
- transitioned operative state;
- available agency, tools, and resources;
- present conditions for action.

### Outputs
- concrete action;
- observable behavior in the world;
- material for later trajectory assessment.

### Risks
- acting from unprocessed urgency;
- substituting activity for movement;
- overcommitting before reality has answered.

### Anti-patterns
- motion as self-soothing;
- execution detached from orientation;
- equating speed with integrity.

## 6. Trajectory

### Purpose
Observe the pattern of motion over time rather than judging the cycle from isolated events.

### Inputs
- sequences of actions;
- temporal observations;
- recurrent responses, deviations, and adaptations.

### Outputs
- a pattern-level reading of motion;
- signs of drift, coherence, correction, or compounding error;
- evidence for whether movement is structurally sound.

Trajectory is not equivalent to progress: local success can still reveal long-run drift, rigidity, or compounding error.

### Risks
- reducing trajectory to a task history;
- inferring direction from too little time or evidence;
- narrating coherence where only fragments exist.

### Anti-patterns
- calling any sequence a trajectory;
- evaluating pattern from a single success or failure;
- using dashboards as substitutes for interpretation.

## 7. Resonance (World)

### Purpose
Test whether the cycle has real correspondence with external reality.

### Inputs
- observed trajectory;
- environmental response;
- consequences, feedback, constraints, and outcomes.

### Outputs
- validation, correction, or contradiction from the world;
- evidence that closes or reopens the loop;
- grounds for the next cycle of self-resonance.

### Risks
- mistaking internal satisfaction for validation;
- filtering out inconvenient feedback;
- closing the cycle without real contact with reality.

### Anti-patterns
- treating agreement inside the system as world resonance;
- cherry-picking evidence;
- declaring completion without external response.

## Architectural notes

- **Resonance (Self)** is the mandatory entry boundary.
- **Resonance (World)** is the mandatory completion boundary.
- If the world does not answer, the loop remains open.
- The protocol does not guarantee that self-reading is accurate.
- The protocol does not remove the possibility of self-deception, collective delusion, or misread feedback.
